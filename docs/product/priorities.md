# What to work on first

Bryan's standing priorities for this product, given to Team Lead on 17
September 2026 and written down here so every session can rank against them
without asking. They order the board. [vision.md](vision.md) says what the
product is for; this file says which of that work goes first.

## The order

1. **Critical stability and effectiveness fixes for the fleet.** Anything that
   stops the fleet working, or makes its work slower or worse, outranks
   everything below. Two things count here and nothing else does:
   - what affects the **speed and quality** of the team's work;
   - what affects **fleet cost**.
2. **New capabilities that significantly push the boundary.** They earn a
   place only when the gain is measurable — several hours a week saved on a
   named activity, or the team staying on high-priority work so that work
   moves measurably faster.

Everything that is neither is below both, however easy it looks.

## How to use it when ranking

- **Rank on the effect, not the effort.** A one-line fix to a cosmetic
  annoyance is still below a week of work on something that blocks daily use.
- **A row has to name its evidence.** A measurement, an incident with a date,
  or a person saying it happened. A row whose case is "this would be nice"
  belongs in the Backlog or nowhere.
- **Say what the gain is in a number.** "Saves the lead a turn per build" is
  rankable; "improves the flow" is not.
- **Never move a row Bryan placed, ranked or wrote.** List it for him instead
  and say what you would have done.
- **The Backlog is not dispatched.** It is where a real but unranked idea
  waits for evidence that it now matters.

## How we know the ranking is wrong

Bryan archiving or deprioritising rows after a sweep is the signal that the
method needs work, not that those rows were unlucky. When it happens, the next
sweep says what the pattern was and what changed in how rows are judged.
